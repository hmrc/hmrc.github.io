Embrace a "you build it, you run it" mindset, ensuring quality, observability and testing are embedded into your processes. Where you do hand over responsibility for your service, ensure it meets the expected standards before doing so.
</p>

<hr class="govuk-section-break--l govuk-section-break--visible">

## Motivation

The people with the most knowledge about a service and how it works are those who built it, therefore they are best placed to support it in a live environment. By continuing to take responsibility for your service in live, you can use qualitative and quantitative feedback to incrementally improve operability and user experience.

<hr class="govuk-section-break--l govuk-section-break--visible">

## Applying this principle

- Ensure you have the knowledge and tools required to manage your own service in production, including access to appropriate data to diagnose and resolve problems.
- Build stakeholder relationships and processes that allow you to manage your own releases, with agreed guard rails or controls for higher risk changes.
- Ensure you have a clear understanding of what standards are expected of you for the tools, technologies and platforms you are using.
- Where you do need to hand over responsibility for your service to another team, collaborate with them to set clear expectations of each other -- preferring conversations and co-working over documentation and checklists.
